Calories in Indian Breakfasts: Poha, Upma, Paratha, Idli and More

Updated 11 November 2026 · By the Thali Lens team

Indian breakfasts vary from 190 kcal to 450 kcal for what looks like one plate. Most are high in carbohydrate and low in protein, which is why hunger returns by eleven.

Carbohydrate first, protein missing

Poha, upma and paratha are made from rice flakes, semolina and wheat. They give energy quickly but only 5 to 9 g of protein. Besan chilla and bread omelette are the two on this list that give a useful amount of protein.

Simple ways to add protein

  • Add roasted peanuts and a bowl of curd to poha.
  • Cook upma with vegetables and have a glass of milk with it.
  • Take extra sambar with idli or dosa.
  • Fill the paratha with paneer or dal instead of only potato.
  • Have a boiled egg or sprouts on the side.

Oil is the swing factor

The same poha can be 200 kcal or 320 kcal depending on the oil in the tadka and the amount of sev on top. Paratha changes even more: dry-roasted with a little ghee brushed on is very different from one shallow-fried in butter.

Fried breakfasts

Puri bhaji, kachori and medu vada are deep fried. They fit well as a weekend treat. As a daily breakfast they bring a lot of fat early in the day.

What keeps you full

Protein and fibre slow digestion. A breakfast with both, for example besan chilla with vegetables and curd, or idli with a full katori of sambar, usually lasts until lunch.
