Calories in Indian Sweets: Gulab Jamun, Jalebi, Ladoo and More

Updated 11 November 2026 · By the Thali Lens team

Mithai is sugar plus fat, usually ghee, khoya or fried dough. A single piece is small, so it is easy to eat three without noticing. The table shows what one piece costs.

Sugar in one piece

The World Health Organization advises keeping added sugar below about 50 g a day for an adult eating 2,000 kcal, and says lower is better. One gulab jamun carries roughly 15 g of sugar. Two pieces after dinner, plus sugar in tea through the day, can cross the limit.

Syrup sweets and dry sweets

Rasgulla and gulab jamun sit in sugar syrup. Pressing a rasgulla lightly before eating removes part of the syrup. Ladoo and barfi have less water, so they are denser: a 40 g besan ladoo has more calories than a 40 g gulab jamun.

Festival season

During Diwali or a wedding week the pieces come from every side. Counting exact calories is not practical. A simple rule works better: one piece at a time, eaten slowly, and not on an empty stomach.

Quick tips

  • Have the sweet right after a meal, when you are already full.
  • Cut large pieces in half and share.
  • Kheer or fruit custard made at home with less sugar is lighter than fried sweets.
  • If you have diabetes, follow the plan your doctor or dietitian has given you.
