Food and exercise together

The food comparison shows scale, not a rule. A samosa is about 250 kcal, which takes most people close to an hour of brisk walking to use. It is far easier to eat calories than to burn them, so the plate matters at least as much as the workout. Exercise still does things food cannot: it protects the heart, muscles, sleep and mood.

To see what is on your plate, analyse a photo of your meal. To find how much you need in a day, use the daily calorie and BMI calculator.

Questions

How are calories burned calculated?

The calculator multiplies a standard intensity value for the activity, called MET, by your body weight in kg and the time in hours. One MET is the energy your body uses at rest.

Why does a heavier person burn more?

Moving a heavier body takes more energy, so the same walk uses more calories for a person of 80 kg than for a person of 55 kg.

How accurate is it?

It is an estimate. Fitness, speed, terrain and age change the real number, and fitness bands often show different values. Use it to compare activities, not as an exact count.
