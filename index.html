How it works

  1. Take or upload a photo, or type the food name. Hold the phone above the plate so every item is visible. If the photo is unclear, type what you ate, for example "2 roti and 1 katori dal". Adding the name along with a photo also improves the result.
  2. The AI reads the plate. It names each dish, estimates the portion in katori, pieces and grams, and lists the likely ingredients.
  3. Read your nutrition report. See calories, protein, carbs, fibre, sugar, fat, sodium, calcium and iron. Change the portion if you ate more or less.

What the report shows

Indian meals are hard to track because one plate holds many dishes and every kitchen cooks differently. Thali Lens breaks the plate into separate items, so you can see that two phulka add about 150 kcal while one katori of paneer butter masala can add more than 300 kcal. For each item you get the portion, the ingredients and the macros. For the whole meal you get total energy, a split of calories from carbohydrate, protein and fat, and each nutrient as a share of an approximate daily reference for an adult eating about 2,000 kcal.

The report also marks the meal as vegetarian or non-vegetarian, lists common allergens such as milk, wheat gluten and nuts, and adds short notes, for example when sodium is high or protein is low.

Tips for a better estimate

  • Keep the whole plate in the frame and avoid heavy shadows.
  • Place a spoon or your hand near the plate so portion size is easier to judge.
  • Photograph gravies before mixing them with rice.
  • If you know extra ghee or butter was added, treat the fat value as a minimum.

Frequently asked questions

How does Thali Lens find calories from a photo?

You upload or click one photo of your meal. An AI vision model recognises each dish, estimates the portion in katori, pieces and grams, and then estimates calories, protein, carbohydrate, fat and other nutrients for that portion.

Which Indian foods can it recognise?

It works with most common home and restaurant food across India: roti, paratha, rice, dal, sabzi, paneer dishes, biryani, dosa, idli, poha, chaat, snacks, sweets and drinks such as chai and lassi. A full thali with several items in one photo also works.

How accurate are the nutrition values?

They are estimates. A photo cannot show how much oil, ghee, butter or sugar was used, so real values can be higher or lower. Use the numbers as a guide, not as a lab report.

Is my photo stored?

No. The photo is resized in your browser, sent once for analysis and not saved on our servers.

Is Thali Lens free?

Yes. The tool is free to use and is supported by advertising.

Can I use it for diabetes or weight loss diet planning?

It can help you understand portion size and carbohydrate load, but it does not replace advice from your doctor or a registered dietitian.
